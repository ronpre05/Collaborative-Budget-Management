# Stakeholder Analysis

There are 4 key stakeholders within the system, the project coordinators, the institutions, their leads and collaborators. This has been written in order to documment who they are, and how we think they will impact the system's development.  

## Analysis

The project coordinators are the people who are going to be creating projects and managing all of the institutions who will be present in the project. They should have the most impact on the system, as they will be creating projects from varying templates, inviting in institutions and editing parts of the budget where needed. It is important that they can do this easily to ensure that collaboration is as simple as possible, and to ensure that merging each individual institutions' budget into the total budget is not a difficult task. It is also important that the software can provide different templates that the coordinator can choose between to ensure that different project types can be dealt with in a single familiar interface. One potential concern is that the software may not end up a simple enough alternative to spreadsheets which people will generally be more familiar with. It is therefore important that the software provides a good ease of use to convince coordinators to utilise it over alternatives. As they have the most use cases of the software, their needs should have the most influence on system design. 

 Following on from them, there are the institutions themselves. The software will affect them as they will need to see what proportion of the funding they are providing and how changes in their allocation may affect the success of the project. For example, if they are providing a large portion of the budget (above 60% in a Horizon RIA project) they may need to reduce their proportion of the funding so that the project can be allowed to proceed. They will likely not directly interact with the software other than to see what proportion of the funding they are providing, and this will most likely be through the institution lead. Therefore, they should not have too much influence on design. 

 Equally as important are the institution leads, who will be a part of an institution who are part of a project. They will need to be able to edit the institutions budget directly, making any necessary changes, but should not be able to change the budget of other institutions. They must be able to do this with ease and see how their changes affect the overall budget. They should expect this to be a simple task as with the project coordinators. They are of lesser priority to project coordinators as they can only change budget allocations for their own institutions, but as they will also commonly use the software, their needs should be considered whilst designing the software. 

 Finally, the institution collaborators may wish the see the budget of the project, but don't need to be able to edit any of the data. It is not a priority that they should be able to, as it doesn't affect them massively, the parts of the budget they are a part of could also be discussed with their institution instead. Generally, they should have little influence on the design as they interact with it the least and can only read information from the software.
